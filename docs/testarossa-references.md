# Ferrari Testarossa — 1986, custom twin mirrors

This is a stylized early Testarossa with a requested pair of matching high mirrors, not the later 512 TR or F512 M. The model uses authored TypeScript geometry, not downloaded car meshes or photographs.

The original reference was the single-mirror [1986 Monospecchio example](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/jg21/lots/r0035-1986-ferrari-testarossa-monospecchio/) documented below. The game now deliberately adds the matching passenger-side housing, support and glass by request. This is a custom twin-high-mirror configuration, not a claim about the photographed car or a conversion to a later model year. The catalog no longer calls the customized car “Monospecchio.”

## Primary specifications

- [Ferrari’s 1986 owner’s manual, scanned/OCR copy](https://manualzz.com/doc/it/5876628/ferrari-testarossa-1986-owner-s-manual), chassis table on printed page 12: 4.485 m overall length, 1.976 m width, 1.130 m height, 2.550 m wheelbase, 1.518/1.660 m front/rear track. This same table is on page 14 of the later US manual.
- [Ferrari’s 1988 US owner’s manual](https://manualzz.com/doc/55732457/ferrari-1988-testarossa--testarossa-owner-s-manual), printed page 16, explicitly lists the 16-inch option: 225/50 VR16 front and 255/50 VR16 rear, alongside the metric TRX option. We use this period 16-inch fitment with the early center-lock five-spoke appearance. Calculated nominal unloaded tire radii are 0.3157 and 0.3307 m; widths are 0.225 and 0.255 m. Wheel animation uses each axle’s own radius.

The 1.025 m front-overhang split and 2.060 m conservative mirror envelope are photograph-derived estimates, not quoted factory measurements. Trim, curvature, window outlines, lamp size and the red paint are art choices rather than manufacturer CAD/paint-code claims. The car keeps the game’s common handling and speed settings.

## Photo comparison

[Iconic Auctioneers’ photographed 1986 single-mirror example](https://www.iconicauctioneers.com/1986-ferrari-testarossa-rec04063-5-0516) supplied two actual profile references ([right side](https://am-s3-bucket-assets.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/silverstone/prod/lot_images/large/rec04063-5/rec04063-5_26.jpg), [left side](https://am-s3-bucket-assets.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/silverstone/prod/lot_images/large/rec04063-5/rec04063-5_25.jpg)). Both were downloaded into ignored research artifacts and visually inspected.

Compared these with the real game garage’s front, side, rear and three-quarter views, including both softness endpoints. Important recognition cues: very low wedge nose, flush pop-up covers, broad rear hips, five horizontal intake strakes along the doors, a compact cabin with long rear buttresses, louvred engine deck, high mirror housings (now paired in the game), rectangular lamps behind the full-width rear grille, five-spoke wheels and four exhaust outlets.

The first garage comparison revealed that triangulated side skins were hiding most of the strakes, and the rear glass was buried in the roof surface. The side skins now follow the concave waist in narrow strips; the rear glass follows its actual roof surface. No research photo is included in the shipped assets. Pop-up covers are shown closed; a hinge animation is not implemented in this pass.
