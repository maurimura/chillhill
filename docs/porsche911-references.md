# Porsche 911 S — 1967 short-wheelbase coupé

The game models the original narrow, long-hood **1967 911 S 2.0 coupé**. It is not a later impact-bumper 911, Carrera RS or Turbo: no ducktail, whale tail, turbo arches or plastic bumper bellows. The model is a hand-built, stylized low-poly interpretation, not a licensed scan or exact CAD reproduction.

## Dimensions and specification

The real car uses a 1,991 cc rear-mounted flat-six, rated at 160 PS, and a five-speed manual with rear-wheel drive. The game deliberately retains its shared, gentle downhill driving model rather than attempting to simulate period engine output, speeds or oversteer.

Reference images are used only for visual research; no downloaded photographs are shipped as game assets. Visual approximations include body curvature, window openings, trim thicknesses, lamp sizes and paint. Tiny trim is slightly exaggerated for legibility in the low-poly driving view.

The original reference's front/right photo above shows no passenger-side mirror, while the [opposite-side photograph](https://cdn.rmsothebys.com/a/8/8/6/9/0/a8869065cfa54403c7c402a8212ab239d1c32609.webp) shows the small round driver's mirror. By request, the game now has matching round chrome mirrors, stems and glass on both sides. This is a deliberate customization of that reference, not a claim about every early 911's equipment.

## Garage review

Compared the integrated model's front, rear, profile and three-quarter garage captures with the real photographs above, including faceted and rounded settings. The first pass exposed two visible problems: the round lamps looked detached from the front fenders, and portions of the indicator/rear-light strips were hidden by body surfaces. The model was revised with painted teardrop headlamp fairings, exterior-mounted light strips and a gentle upward sweep to the front/rear lower valances. A second comparison reduced the exaggerated headlamp diameter and nose height to match the photos' proportions more closely. The Fuchs-style wheel spokes were widened in the shared wheel builder to better suggest the period five-petal design.

Review targets: open arches with visible tires; circular raised headlights; a low center bonnet; continuous fastback roof rather than a sedan trunk; triangular quarter windows; narrow chrome bumpers; discrete rear lamps; conforming engine grille; and no modern aero parts. These are intentionally stylized and small-scale trim remains approximate.
